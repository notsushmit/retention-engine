## Methods and limits

- The numbers come from one run on macOS arm64 (Python 3.11.17, numpy 2.4.6, LightGBM 4.7.0, scikit-learn 1.9.1).
- The 30-day test rests on one test month (2017-01); the 7-day test has two (2017-01 and 2017-02); the 59-day demonstration has one.
- This interval covers which customers happened to be in that month, not month-to-month change. The bootstrap draws customers with replacement; a drawn customer brings all their rows.
- The calibration limits (slope and intercept) and the 0.01 minimum difference were fixed in advance; no probability is recalibrated on test data.
- Cox candidates cox02 and cox04 (ridge penalty 0.01) failed to fit: the optimiser diverged on customers whose last row is a trial, ending with a log-likelihood below the model without inputs. They are excluded, so Cox has two usable candidates (cox01 and cox03).
- The proportional-hazards test ran on a fixed subsample of customers (md5 buckets 0-1 of the sample), never on all fit rows, because its cost grows faster than quadratically with rows; it is descriptive.
- Each calibration interval is labelled not computable only when its own regression fails on the original data or on a replicate: the slope interval uses the two-parameter fit, the intercept interval the fit with the slope fixed at 1. The calibration verdict uses point estimates; an interval only marks a pass or fail as borderline.
- The calibration half of 2016-10 and the selection half share the month's conditions; the split separates customers, not months (a mild dependence).
- Classifier hyperparameters at 30 and 59 days were chosen on one fold (f30), a weaker guard than the two 7-day folds.
- No calibration plot was logged: the run holds slope, intercept and ECE, not the per-bin values a reliability plot needs, so none is drawn here.
- Each calibration limit failure is read from the point estimates, as registered; the intervals only mark it borderline.
- Exploratory, post hoc checks (anything not registered, such as the 2016 rehearsal months or score means) are kept out of this report and labelled exploratory wherever they appear; none changes a registered value or verdict.
- The month-level data-quality screen can miss a single missing day: its batch indicator looks only at the month's last day, and the cohort indicators can stay under their limits. A daily batch-size indicator is needed for drift monitoring.
- Exploratory, post hoc (read-only, 2016 data): on 2016-12-05 the sample has 1,991 auto-renew payment rows against 4,929 to 5,254 on the 5th of September to November 2016. Of the 5,430 7-day lapses of the 2016-12-01 snapshot (P2, main segments), 3,339 paid again in January 2017; 3,129 of those had coverage ending on 2016-12-05 and 3,092 paid on 2017-01-05, a 31-day gap, one day past the 30-day renewal window. 2016-12-01 is not a test month and was never a usable 7-day snapshot; none of these customers is a row of the 2017-01-01 snapshot. It was used as a stand-in month in a rehearsal of this stage, and those rehearsal numbers are not reported.
- Exploratory, post hoc (daily counts of auto-renew payment rows, rule written before the counts were read; a day is a gap below 60% of the median of the same day over the three preceding months): gap days in 2016-09 to 2017-03 are 2016-12-05 (ratio 0.395), 2016-12-30 (0.013, an artifact of the rule: the month-end batch fell on the 31st, with 38,508 rows) and 2017-02-13 (2,080 rows against a baseline of 4,822; ratio 0.431). The renewals deciding a test month's 7-day outcomes are dated from T to T + 37 days: 2017-01-01 to 2017-02-07 for 2017-01 (no gap day) and 2017-02-01 to 2017-03-10 for 2017-02 (contains 2017-02-13). The 2017-02 7-day cell could be affected by missing renewals: missing renewals make renewing customers look lapsed, so the direction is upward for its observed rate; the size is not estimated. Reasoning, not shown: the same day also lies in the renewal windows of the 2017-01 30-day cell and the 59-day demonstration. No verdict or number in this report changes.
